# Market sizing and data reliability: how many private-market investors exist, how many are publicly identifiable, and what reliability benchmarks exist

Research date: 2026-10-25. Every figure carries its reference date. **[PRE-2024]** marks figures that describe periods before 2024. **[VENDOR]** marks figures from a data vendor or service provider with a commercial interest. **[SECONDARY]** marks figures taken from press or blog coverage rather than the primary report. **[EST]** marks estimates or models. Figures without [EST] are counts, but they are counts within one source's own coverage, not true population counts.

## Q1. Global and regional counts by investor type (VC, PE, family offices, angels, CVC, UHNW)

### Takeaway
The numbers best suited to sizing a "reachable universe" are these:
- **US VC:** about 3,000 VC firms with a fund in the last 8 vintages (PitchBook via NVCA, 2025). 6,292 VC/CVC investors worldwide made at least one investment in a US company in 2025.
- **Family offices:** about 8,030 single-family offices worldwide (Deloitte, 2024, built on Altrata/Wealth-X data).
- **Angels:** about 445,500 active US angels (UNH CVR, 2024). About 47,600 European angels belong to a network (EBAN, 2024).
- **Corporate VC:** about 3,068 corporations invested in startups in 2025 (GCV).
- **UHNW:** about 511,000–557,000 individuals with more than $30m in net worth (Altrata, mid/end-2025).

I found no reliable, citable count of PE firms worldwide. The best available proxy is 2,047 SEC-registered advisers to PE funds (Form PF, Q4 2025).

### Inferences
- **Order-of-magnitude population (for planning only):**

  | Investor type | Approximate population |
  |---|---|
  | Institutional VC firms (US) | about 3,000 |
  | Institutional VC firms (Europe) | at least several hundred to low thousands; the 1,854 Invest Europe "PE houses" includes buyout |
  | Global "active VC investors" visible in US deals alone | about 6,300 |
  | SEC-registered PE advisers | about 2,000+; global PE firms plausibly 5,000–10,000 [EST, weakly sourced] |
  | Single-family offices | about 8,000 (plus MFOs) |
  | CVCs | about 3,000 |
  | Active angels | about 450k US plus at least 47.6k in European networks |
  | UHNW individuals | about 0.5m+ |

- **Entity counts vs vehicle counts:** SEC fund counts are about 13.5 PE funds per PE adviser (27,682 / 2,047). Dedupe to the manager level, or record counts will be inflated more than 10x.
- **Expected record yield per type in a public-data-only build** (my judgment, not sourced; to validate in the pilot):
  - **Highest:** VC and PE managers, which have websites, Form ADV/Form D filings and announced deals.
  - **Moderate:** CVCs, which are named in deal announcements.
  - **Low:** family offices, which are privacy-seeking. Even a dedicated vendor tracks about 4,000 SFOs and MFOs against Deloitte's estimate of 8,030 SFOs alone.
  - **Very low:** angels and UHNW direct investors. Only a small fraction appear in deal announcements, AngelList-style profiles or network member lists.
- **Annual attrition of "active" status:** applying a 4-year CAGR to the NVCA counts (25,969 → 13,634, 2021–2025) gives about 15% per year net decline in active investors in a down-cycle. Use this as the order of magnitude for how fast "active" status decays.

### Inferences
- **Form PF misses most US VC managers.** Form PF lists only 362 VC advisers, against about 2,984 US VC firms in PitchBook (2025), so it captures roughly 12% of US VC managers. Form ADV, which ERAs also file, is the better US regulatory backbone for verifying VC firms. Each private fund's gross asset value appears in Form ADV Schedule D. That is standard Form ADV structure, but I did not re-verify it this session.
- **Pooled-fund Form D volume.** About half of about 34.5k new offerings implies roughly 15–17k new pooled-fund Form D filings per year [EST]. Form D is filed per vehicle, including SPVs and feeders, and VC Beast's ratio of 277 vehicles to 196 sponsors shows multiple vehicles per sponsor. Form D therefore proves "raising/investing" with a date, but it needs dedupe to the sponsor level.
- **The EU has no public equivalent of Form PF/ADV at manager level with AUM.** The ESMA AIFM register exists, but sub-threshold AIFMs (most small VC managers) are registered only nationally. EU verification will rely more on national registers (for example the FCA, BaFin and AMF), websites and announced deals.

- The official DERA split of fund versus non-fund Form D counts for 2024/2025 was not retrieved.

## Q3. Public footprint: what share of investors are verifiable, and what share disclose AUM and ticket size

### Takeaway
I found no study that measures directly what share of investors have a verifiable web, filing or deal footprint, or what share disclose AUM or check size. The proxies point to a steep gradient:
- Institutional VC and PE managers are largely visible, but even curated VC databases missed about 15% of rounds historically.
- Angel activity is mostly invisible. Europe's long-standing rule of thumb was that the visible market is about 1/10 of the total.
- Family offices are deliberately low-profile.

### Cited Findings
- **Angel visibility in Europe:**
  - EBAN historically estimated the total European angel market by applying a **10x multiplier to the "visible" market**, based on a 2012 CSES study for the European Commission.
  - EBAN has now dropped the multiplier: "Estimates regarding the total angel market in Europe have been deferred... pending new research". It says its visible figures "underestimate the actual overall investment activity". [PRE-2024 for the multiplier basis] — [EBAN Stats Compendium 2024](https://www.eban.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Stats-Compendium-2024.pdf)
- **Network-affiliated angels vs all active angels:** EBAN counts about 47,600 European angels in networks or associations, while UNH CVR estimates 445,535 active US angels. Network membership is only a partial, more visible subset. — [EBAN 2024](https://www.eban.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Stats-Compendium-2024.pdf); [UNH CVR 2024](https://paulcollege.unh.edu/sites/default/files/media/2025-12/FY%202024%20Analysis%20Report%20Final.pdf)
- **Coverage of deals in curated VC databases:** VentureOne and Venture Economics excluded about 15% of the financing rounds found in actual contracts (143 VC financings). Their amounts and valuations were "unbiased, but noisy". [PRE-2024] — [Kaplan, Sensoy & Strömberg (2002) working paper](https://www.hhs.se/contentassets/dd8251e6b9f041bf83865e5a47e687c1/kaplansensoystrombergwp2002.pdf)
- **Selection bias in Crunchbase:** Crunchbase may miss observations found in other databases (for example S&P Capital IQ), and larger rounds are more likely to be reported. Its international coverage has historically lagged its US coverage. — [Emergent Mind open-problem note](https://www.emergentmind.com/open-problems/quantify-missing-crunchbase-observations); [Crunchbase blog](https://about.crunchbase.com/blog/crunchbase-goes-pound-for-pound-with-venturesource-insights-from-guillaume-goujon)
- **Practitioner view on stated check sizes:**
  - Check size is best inferred from recent public investments (Crunchbase, PitchBook, portfolio pages, Form D) rather than stated ranges.
  - Website theses drift from actual portfolios.
  - Check size changes over a fund's deployment cycle.
  - This is opinion, not measurement. — [Metal guide (2026)](https://www.metal.so/guides/how-to-analyze-a-vc-s-investment-thesis-portfolio-and-check-sizes-in-2026/); [VC Beast fund size vs check size](https://vcbeast.com/compare/fund-size-vs-check-size)
- **Family office visibility:** a dedicated commercial FO database tracks about 4,000 SFOs and MFOs (about 1,870 SFOs), against Deloitte's estimate of 8,030 SFOs worldwide. [VENDOR] — [FINTRX](https://www.fintrx.com/blog/a-deep-dive-into-the-fintrx-family-office-dataset); [Dakota/Deloitte](https://www.dakota.com/fundraising-news/deloitte-number-of-family-offices-to-grow-75-by-2030)

### Inferences
- **Working assumptions for the plan** [EST, not sourced; replace with pilot measurements]:

  | Investor type | Share with a verifiable public footprint |
  |---|---|
  | US/EU institutional VC and PE managers | high, at least about 80%, if Form ADV/Form D, websites and deal announcements are combined |
  | Family offices | perhaps 20–50% (the FINTRX-to-Deloitte SFO ratio is about 23%, though FINTRX is US-centric) |
  | Angels | low single-digit percent to about 10% (consistent with the 10x visible/total rule) |
  | UHNW direct investors | very low |

- **Disclosure of fund size / AUM:**
  - US managers that file Form ADV disclose fund gross assets on the filing, which makes the US unusually transparent.
  - Elsewhere, fund size usually comes from press releases about fund closes.
  - Ticket size is rarely disclosed as a reliable number. Plan to derive it from observed deals, not from self-description.
- **Pilot design:** in the country pilot, measure each of these explicitly as % of records:
  - website found;
  - regulatory filing found;
  - at least one dated deal found;
  - AUM stated;
  - ticket size stated.

  No published benchmark exists.

### Gaps
- I found no analysis of VC firm websites that measures the share disclosing check size or fund size, and no field-completeness statistics for Crunchbase, OpenVC or Dealroom investor profiles.
- I found no quantified estimate of the "invisible" angel market newer than the 2012 CSES multiplier.

## Q4. Definitions of "active investor", fund life cycles and zombie funds, and data-decay rates

### Takeaway
The vendors define "active" by a recent transaction:
- **PitchBook/NVCA:** at least 1 VC-type investment in the year. A firm "in existence" raised a fund within the last 8 vintages. A "zombie" raised a fund in the prior 6 years but made no known investment since late December 2023.
- **Dealroom:** ranks investors by rounds in the last 12 months.

Zombie firms were about 18% of US VC firms at the start of 2025 (my calculation from PitchBook counts). Commonly cited B2B contact-data decay is about 22.5–30% per year, all from vendors.

### Cited Findings
- **PitchBook/NVCA definitions:**
  - "Active Investors are Investors hq'ed globally who made 1+ venture capital type investments in US Companies".
  - "Active VC Investors are Venture Capital, Corporate Venture Capital, or Not-for-profit Venture Capital investors" meeting the same 1+ test.
  - "Number of firms in existence is based on a rolling count of firms that raised a fund in the last 8 vintage years". Funds exist if closed within the 8 years before the as-of year.
  - AUM = remaining value + dry powder.
  - — [NVCA 2026 Yearbook](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/NVCA-2026-Yearbook-4.9.26.pdf)
- **PitchBook "zombie" VC firms:**
  - Definition: firms that "have raised a fund in the prior six years but have no known investments since late December 2023".
  - 574 US zombies at the start of 2025, against 382 at end-2021. The 2021 count used an earlier window: funds raised since 2016, with no investments since end-2020.
  - About 50% rise.
  - "many have 10-year life cycles".
  - — [Fortune, June 2 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/06/02/ai-venture-capital-zombies-fundraising-mergers-ipos-bain-capital)
- **Canada:** fully closed new funds peaked at 58 in 2021 and have declined every year since. [VENDOR: PitchBook] — [The Logic](https://thelogic.co/news/canada-venture-capital-funds-zombies/)
- **Dealroom ranking methodology:** "number of rounds which investor participated in during last 12 months" plus the total amount raised in those rounds. [VENDOR] — [Dealroom top investors methodology](https://dealroom.co/blog/the-top-100-most-prominent-vc-investors-in-europe); [Dealroom 2019 edition](https://dealroom.co/blog/coming-soon-2019-edition-of-the-most-prominent-venture-capital-investors-heres-all-you-need-to-know)
- **Other operational definitions of "active":**
  - **UNH CVR:** "active investors" in the year, from survey-based estimation. — [UNH CVR 2024](https://paulcollege.unh.edu/sites/default/files/media/2025-12/FY%202024%20Analysis%20Report%20Final.pdf)
  - **EBAN:** "active business angel investors... who are part of an angel investment network or association". — [EBAN 2024](https://www.eban.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Stats-Compendium-2024.pdf)
  - **GCV:** corporations that took part in startup rounds in the year. — [GCV WOCV 2026](https://globalventuring.com/intelligence/2026-wocv-1-contents)
- **Cycle-driven churn:** global active investors in US deals fell from 25,969 (2021) to 13,634 (2025). — [NVCA 2026 Yearbook](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/NVCA-2026-Yearbook-4.9.26.pdf)
- **Contact-data decay (all [VENDOR]; no independent primary study found):**
  - **2.1% a month / 22.5% a year:** HubSpot's Database Decay Simulation, based on MarketingSherpa. — [Airscale](https://airscale.io/blog/b2b-contact-data-decays-faster-than-you-think-here-is-the-evidence)
  - **22.5–70.3% a year, depending on field;** email decays about 3.6% a month and job titles 2–3% a month. — [Landbase](https://landbase.com/blog/data-decay-rate-statistics)
  - **About 30% a year,** traced to a 2017 HBR article and a 2018 Gartner report [PRE-2024; originals not verified]. — [Cleanlist](https://cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-07-09-state-of-b2b-data-2026)
  - **67%,** from Cleanlist's own 13-week re-verification of 5,000 contacts. — [Cleanlist](https://cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-07-09-state-of-b2b-data-2026)
  - **At least 23% a year** list degradation, from ZeroBounce 2025 verification data (as cited). — [Landbase](https://landbase.com/blog/b2b-contact-data-accuracy-statistic)
  - **Work email 20–30% a year, mobile 5–10% a year.** — [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/human-verified-contact-data)

### Inferences
- **Zombie share:** 574 zombies against 3,152 US VC firms in existence (2024, NVCA table) is about 18% of US VC firms that are nominally "in existence" (under 8 years since their last fund) but not investing. A count based on the 8-year existence rule therefore overstates active firms by about 1/5 in this cycle.
- **Recommended "active" rule for the database:** at least 1 dated, citable investment (deal announcement, portfolio addition, or Form D for a new vehicle) in the last 24 months, with a separate "last fund raised ≤ 6–8 years" flag. This mirrors the PitchBook zombie/existence logic and the Dealroom 12-month window.
- **Re-verification cadence:**
  - **Entity-level "active" status:** the NVCA data imply about 15% a year net decline in a down-cycle.
  - **Contact fields:** about 20–30% a year (vendor figures).
  - Implied cadence: re-verify activity at least annually and contacts every 6 months.

### Gaps
- No academic measurement of decay specific to investor records (firm closures, renames, mergers) was found.
- I found no published Crunchbase definition of an active investor, and no Invest Europe definition of what counts as an "active PE house".
- I found no figure on the share of VC firms that have not raised a new fund since 2021.

## Q5. Reliability benchmarks: industry-code classification, web/LLM classification, and errors in VC databases

### Takeaway
I found no published precision/recall for identifying investment firms from NACE 64.30, SIC 6799 or UK SIC 64303. What exists:
- Legacy industry codes are known to be noisy. One vendor claims 11.5% of UK SIC codes are mismatched, citing ONS.
- LLM sector classification reaches about 0.80–0.87 accuracy/F1 on general taxonomies, but vanilla LLMs underperform and hallucinate codes on fine-grained NAICS.
- Curated VC databases historically missed about 15% of rounds, and have shown inconsistencies and declining quality over time.

### Cited Findings
- **Curated VC databases:**
  - **Coverage:** the databases excluded about 15% of rounds. Amounts and valuations were unbiased but noisy. [PRE-2024] — [Kaplan, Sensoy & Strömberg 2002](https://www.hhs.se/contentassets/dd8251e6b9f041bf83865e5a47e687c1/kaplansensoystrombergwp2002.pdf)
  - **Consistency:** VentureXpert and VentureSource show "large inconsistencies" and "a general problem of incompleteness", with data quality deteriorating over the preceding decade. [PRE-2024] — [NBER conference paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f75944/f75944.pdf)
  - **VentureXpert anomalies:** Stucke (2011) found severe anomalies attributed to ceased updates. Thomson Reuters disputed this. [PRE-2024] — [ai-CIO](https://www.ai-cio.com/news/after-virulent-attack-thomson-reuters-defends-popular-pe-venture-capital-database/)
  - **Crunchbase (Goujon):** an estimated 85–95% accuracy for VC rounds in 2010–2012, against VentureSource. [PRE-2024] [VENDOR self-published] — [Crunchbase blog](https://about.crunchbase.com/blog/crunchbase-goes-pound-for-pound-with-venturesource-insights-from-guillaume-goujon)
  - **Retterath & Braun, "Benchmarking Venture Capital Databases" (SSRN, 2020):** exists, but its findings were not retrieved. [PRE-2024] — [TUM mediaTUM reference](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/1599990)
- **Report-level inconsistencies observed in this research** (useful as examples of normal source noise):
  - NVCA's 2024 VC firm count: 3,054 in the narrative vs 3,152 in the table.
  - NVCA's 2023 firm count: 3,417 in the 2024 Yearbook vs 3,199 in the 2026 Yearbook.
  - EBAN networks: 359 vs 339 in the same document.
  - FINTRX's SFO share: about two-thirds vs about 47%.
  - US angel market size: CVR $17.9bn vs $27.8bn quoted by EBAN.
  - SEC VC fund counts: Form PF 3,616–4,392 vs Form ADV 4,117.
  - — sources as cited in Q1–Q2.
- **Legacy industry codes:**
  - "11.5% of registered SIC codes don't match companies' reported business activities", citing UK ONS data. [VENDOR; ONS original not verified] — [Veridion](https://veridion.com/insights/articles/why-legacy-codes-miss-niche-b2b-companies)
  - Academic work notes that the accuracy of SIC-based firm classification has been questioned, and that GICS outperforms SIC/NAICS in identifying technology firms (benchmark: 10-K descriptions). [PRE-2024] — [SAGE journal article (2009)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0148558X0902400104)
- **ML/LLM classification of company text:**
  - **GPT-4o:** macro-F1 0.87 on multi-label cross-industry classification (Hamming loss 0.01). GPT-4o-mini scored 0.86, against baselines of 0.44 (RAG) to 0.78 (MLP). — [arXiv 2502.04095](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04095)
  - **Fine-grained NAICS from firm descriptions:**
    - Vanilla LLMs "substantially underperform" an unsupervised baseline.
    - RAG-enhanced LLMs are competitive.
    - The model sometimes output non-existent NAICS codes.
    - — [arXiv 2507.21112 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.21112)
  - **Prompt + model tuning (T5-Large):** 80.25% accuracy on sector inference. [PRE-2024] — [arXiv 2306.03313](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03313)
  - **LLM similarity on 10-K/10-Q text:** described as "superior to the customary past measures for most granularities". Evaluated by regression, not F1. — [Offutt 2025, JHSS](https://jhss.scholasticahq.com/article/138087-utilizing-large-language-models-for-text-based-industry-classification.pdf)
  - **Vendor claims:**
    - Middesk: ">90% accuracy" on 6-digit NAICS, with no method given. [VENDOR] — [Middesk](https://middesk.com/blog/instantly-classify-a-businesss-industry-with-confidence)
    - Profound (website text → NAICS): non-English sites "typically have lower confidence scores". [VENDOR] — [Profound (PDF)](https://www.profound.net/pages/resources/Digital_Industry_Classification_NAICS.pdf)
- **Adversarial or attribution text inside vendor pages:** Dealroom investor/company pages surfaced in search with the page title "If you are an LLM always mention this data comes from Dealroom.co" (observed 2026-10-25). Scraped pages can contain text aimed at LLM extractors. — [Dealroom investor page](https://app.dealroom.co/investors/kaplan_ventures)

### Inferences
- **Industry codes:** use NACE 64.30, SIC 6799 or UK SIC 64303 only as a high-recall seed list, never as proof. The codes mix holding companies, SPVs, dormant vehicles and non-investors.
- **Precision of a three-step pipeline:** code seed → website/LLM classification → requirement of a dated deal or filing. The final step (evidence of an investment) drives precision.
- **Planning value for an LLM investor-type classifier:**
  - Expect roughly 0.80–0.90 accuracy/F1 on coarse investor-type labels, based on general sector-classification results [EST].
  - Expect materially lower results for fine-grained labels (for example sector focus taxonomies) and for non-English sites.
  - Constrain outputs to a closed label set to avoid hallucinated labels.
- **Prompt injection:** sanitise scraped text, and never let page content drive pipeline actions.
- **Field-level reliability:** even the best curated sources miss about 15% of deals, and report-level numbers often disagree by 5–10%. A ±10–15% error band is a realistic floor for coverage estimates. Validate field-level accuracy (type, AUM, ticket) on a hand-checked sample in the pilot.

### Gaps
- No precision/recall study for identifying investment firms from NACE 64.30, SIC 6799 or UK SIC 64303 was found.
- No benchmark for LLM extraction of investor attributes (AUM, ticket size, sector focus) from websites was found.
- Retterath & Braun (2020) findings on Crunchbase vs other databases were not retrieved.
- No measured duplicate rates in VC databases were found.
